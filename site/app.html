What this is

Every polling place in Victoria, with its results from the 2014, 2018 and 2022 state elections and the federal elections from 2010 to 2025, pinned to a map coordinate so the vote can be followed by place rather than by electorate. Boundaries change; a school hall does not.

How to read it

Dots are election-day booths, sized by votes cast there. Catchments are a model: each booth is given the area closer to it than to any other booth in the same district, so the map fills in. Early, postal and absent votes are not tied to a booth by the electoral commissions and are shown at district level in the booth panel only. In 2022 about half of all state votes were cast before election day, so a booth map shows election-day voters, not everyone.

Switch from Booths to Electorates to see whole-seat results, which include every vote type and are what decide who is elected. Coalition means Liberal plus National; One Nation and the Victorian Socialists have their own colours; Independents are everyone running without a registered party. Shares are of the formal vote.

Where the numbers come from

Victorian Electoral Commission results pages and spreadsheets (the 2018 pages via the Internet Archive, as the VEC no longer serves them); Australian Electoral Commission Tally Room downloads; Vicmap and AEC boundary files. Coordinates come from AEC polling place data where a state booth matches a federal premises by name, otherwise from OpenStreetMap at locality level, flagged as approximate. Every district’s booth totals reconcile to the published district totals, with one documented exception (Ripon 2018, where the only booth-level count published predates the recount).
