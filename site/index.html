What this is

Every polling place, election after election: results pinned to the place the votes were cast, so the vote can be followed by school hall and church porch rather than by electorate. Boundaries change; a school hall does not. It starts with Victoria — the state elections since 2010 and the federal elections since 2010 (House and Senate) — and is built to take on other states and the federal map in the same way.

How to read it

Dots are election-day booths, sized by votes cast there. Catchments are a model: each booth is given the area closer to it than to any other booth in the same district, so the map fills in. Early, postal and absent votes are not tied to a booth by the electoral commissions and are shown at district level in the booth panel only. In 2022 about half of all state votes were cast before election day, so a booth map shows election-day voters, not everyone.

The Dashboard is the statewide view of any election: primary vote by party with the change since last time, the seat tally against the majority line, a seat spectrum (one box per seat from the strongest Labor seat to the strongest Coalition seat, by primary leader or after preferences), and every seat with who held it, who won or leads, the margin and the 2CP swing, filterable to the seats changing hands or in doubt. On election night it fills in by itself from the live feed; seats are called by this page’s own rule (a clear projected lead for the share of booths in) and marked declared only when the VEC publishes the distribution.

Polls is a separate compilation of published opinion polling for the 2026 election — statewide primaries and two-party figures with a smoothed trend, three-party splits, regional breakdowns, the MRP seat projections and leadership ratings — taken from Wikipedia’s polling page. It is for reading the campaign; nothing from it feeds the Dashboard or the election-night projections.

Demographics puts the 2021 Census beside the vote: every seat as a dot, any census indicator across, any of the map’s measures up (primary share, 2CP, swings, leader), with a fitted line and the correlation, and a second chart showing how that correlation has moved from election to election. The census figures come from the Parliament of Victoria Library’s electorate data cards for the 2022 boundaries and from the ABS Census tables for the districts as drawn in 2013 (the 2014 and 2018 elections) and the federal divisions of 2021 (the 2022 election), so each election is read against the boundaries it was fought on; other elections are matched by seat name. Every seat’s panel carries a short “who lives here” table against the Victorian figure. One caution on the 2021 figures: the Census was taken during Melbourne’s August 2021 lockdown, so anything about travel to work describes that week.

Switch from Booths to Electorates to see whole-seat results, which include every vote type and are what decide who is elected. Coalition means Liberal plus National; One Nation and the Victorian Socialists have their own colours; Independents are everyone running without a registered party. Shares are of the formal vote.

Where the numbers come from

Victorian Electoral Commission results pages and spreadsheets (the 2018 pages via the Internet Archive, as the VEC no longer serves them); Australian Electoral Commission Tally Room downloads; Vicmap and AEC boundary files. Coordinates come from AEC polling place data where a state booth matches a federal premises by name, otherwise from OpenStreetMap at locality level, flagged as approximate. Every district’s booth totals reconcile to the published district totals, with one documented exception (Ripon 2018, where the only booth-level count published predates the recount). Narracan’s 2022 contest was deferred to a supplementary election on 28 January 2023 after a candidate died during the campaign; it is shown as part of 2022 (the 88th seat of that parliament) and flagged wherever it appears, including in the contests and booths tables under Query.

Pipeline, raw data and reconciliation checks: the election-analyser project folder.

Privacy

The public site counts visits with GoatCounter: which tab was opened and roughly where the visit came from, nothing more. No cookies, no personal data, no advertising, and nothing is counted when the atlas is run from a local copy. Map tiles are served by OpenFreeMap.
